# Provenance Guard - Project Planning

## System Overview

Provenance Guard is a backend system designed to analyze text-based creative content and estimate whether the content is likely human-written or AI-generated. The system uses multiple detection signals rather than relying on a single method. Each submission receives an attribution result, confidence score, and transparency label. Creators who believe their work was incorrectly classified can submit an appeal, which changes the content status to under review and records the appeal in the audit log.

## Detection Signals

### Signal 1: LLM-Based Classification

The first detection signal uses the Groq API with an LLM to evaluate the submitted text. The model examines characteristics such as writing style, structure, consistency, phrasing, and overall patterns that may indicate AI-generated writing.

The signal returns an AI-likelihood score between 0.0 and 1.0. A score closer to 1.0 indicates that the LLM considers the text more likely to be AI-generated, while a score closer to 0.0 indicates that it considers the text more likely to be human-written.

Limitation: An LLM classifier may incorrectly identify polished or highly formal human writing as AI-generated. It may also have difficulty identifying AI-generated text that has been substantially edited by a human.

### Signal 2: Stylometric Heuristics

The second signal uses Python-based stylometric analysis. It examines measurable characteristics of the writing, including sentence-length variation, vocabulary diversity, and punctuation usage. These characteristics provide a structural signal that is independent from the LLM's semantic assessment.

The stylometric analysis returns an AI-likelihood score between 0.0 and 1.0. More uniform writing patterns increase the AI-likelihood score, while greater variation generally decreases it.

Limitation: Some human writers naturally write in a consistent or repetitive style, while AI-generated content can be edited to introduce irregularities. Therefore, stylometric measurements cannot reliably determine authorship on their own.

### Combining the Signals

The two signals will be combined into one AI-likelihood score using a weighted average:

`combined_score = (llm_score * 0.60) + (stylometric_score * 0.40)`

The LLM signal receives slightly more weight because it evaluates the text holistically, while the stylometric signal provides an independent structural check. Neither signal is used alone to make the final classification.

## Uncertainty Representation

The combined score represents the system's estimate of how strongly the available signals indicate AI-generated writing.

The classification ranges will be:

- **0.00-0.34:** Likely Human
- **0.35-0.69:** Uncertain
- **0.70-1.00:** Likely AI

For example, a score of 0.60 does not mean the system has proven that the content is AI-generated. It means the signals provide mixed evidence, so the system will classify the content as uncertain.

The intentionally wide uncertainty range helps reduce false accusations against human creators. A high threshold is required before content receives a likely-AI classification because falsely labeling human-created work as AI-generated can negatively affect creator trust and attribution.

## Transparency Label Design

The transparency label communicates the classification result and confidence level in plain language so that a non-technical reader can understand what the system determined. The system will use three label variants.

### High-Confidence AI

**Exact label text:**

> "Likely AI-Generated: Our analysis found strong indicators that this content may have been generated by AI. This classification is based on multiple detection signals and is not a definitive determination of authorship."

This label is displayed when the combined AI-likelihood score is between 0.70 and 1.00.

### High-Confidence Human

**Exact label text:**

> "Likely Human-Written: Our analysis found strong indicators that this content was written by a human. This classification is based on multiple detection signals and is not a definitive determination of authorship."

This label is displayed when the combined AI-likelihood score is between 0.00 and 0.34.

### Uncertain

**Exact label text:**

> "Uncertain: Our analysis found mixed indicators and cannot confidently determine whether this content was human-written or AI-generated."

This label is displayed when the combined AI-likelihood score is between 0.35 and 0.69.

## Appeals Workflow

Creators can appeal a classification when they believe their content has been incorrectly labeled.

An appeal must include:

- `content_id`: the unique identifier assigned to the original submission.
- `creator_reasoning`: the creator's explanation of why they believe the classification is incorrect.

When an appeal is submitted:

1. The system receives the `content_id` and `creator_reasoning` through the `POST /appeal` endpoint.
2. The system locates the original classification associated with the content ID.
3. The content status changes from `classified` to `under_review`.
4. The creator's reasoning is stored alongside the original classification.
5. The appeal is added to the structured audit log.
6. The system returns a confirmation that the appeal was received.

A human reviewer would be able to see the original content ID, attribution result, confidence score, individual detection signal scores, creator reasoning, submission timestamp, and current review status.

The system will not automatically reclassify appealed content. The appeal places the classification under review for later human evaluation.

## Anticipated Edge Cases

### Edge Case 1: Highly Formal Human Writing

Academic, professional, or highly polished human writing may contain consistent sentence structures and formal vocabulary that resemble patterns associated with AI-generated text. Both the LLM and stylometric signal could therefore produce an unusually high AI-likelihood score for genuine human writing.

The system reduces the potential harm of this case by requiring a combined score of at least 0.70 before displaying the likely-AI label, providing an uncertainty category, and allowing the creator to appeal the result.

### Edge Case 2: Human-Edited AI Content

A creator may substantially edit AI-generated text by adding personal language, changing sentence structures, introducing informal wording, and varying punctuation. These changes may cause the stylometric signal to identify human-like variation even though AI was involved in creating the original content.

Because Provenance Guard analyzes the submitted text rather than its complete creation history, the system may classify heavily edited AI content as uncertain or likely human.

### Edge Case 3: Creative Writing With Unusual Structure

Poetry, experimental writing, or intentionally repetitive creative work may contain short sentences, repeated words, unusual punctuation, or limited vocabulary. Stylometric heuristics could interpret these intentional artistic choices as indicators of AI-generated writing.

The multi-signal approach reduces reliance on these structural measurements by also considering the independent LLM-based signal.

## Architecture

### Submission Flow

```text
                         Raw Text + Creator ID
                                  |
                                  v
                           POST /submit
                                  |
                    +-------------+-------------+
                    |                           |
                    v                           v
          LLM-Based Classification     Stylometric Analysis
               (Groq API)                 (Pure Python)
                    |                           |
                    | llm_score                 | stylometric_score
                    +-------------+-------------+
                                  |
                                  v
                         Confidence Scoring
                    60% LLM + 40% Stylometric
                                  |
                                  v
                         Attribution Result
                                  |
                                  v
                         Transparency Label
                                  |
                                  v
                            Audit Log
                                  |
                                  v
                           JSON Response
          (content_id, attribution, confidence, label)
```

### Appeal Flow

```text
                Content ID + Creator Reasoning
                              |
                              v
                       POST /appeal
                              |
                              v
                   Locate Original Decision
                              |
                              v
                 Status = "under_review"
                              |
                              v
                    Update Audit Log
                              |
                              v
                    Confirmation Response
```

A submission enters the system through `POST /submit`, where the raw text is analyzed independently by the LLM-based classifier and stylometric heuristic signal. Their scores are combined into a single confidence score, which determines the attribution result and transparency label before the complete decision is written to the audit log and returned to the user.

Appeals enter through `POST /appeal` using the original `content_id` and the creator's reasoning. The system locates the original decision, changes its status to `under_review`, records the appeal in the audit log, and returns confirmation to the creator.
